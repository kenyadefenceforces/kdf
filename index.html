Honouring our Defence Forces

KDF Day: 14 October

Every year on 14 October, Kenya marks Kenya Defence Forces Day, a day set aside to honour the courage, discipline and sacrifice of the women and men who serve in the Kenya Army, the Kenya Air Force and the Kenya Navy. The day has been observed since 2012 and recalls the launch of Operation Linda Nchi in October 2011. Last month the Forces marked KDF Day 2026 with ceremonies, remembrance of fallen comrades and service to communities. To every soldier, airman, sailor and member of the support services, whether deployed or at home, and to the families who stand behind you: congratulations, and thank you for guarding Kenya’s sovereignty, responding in times of disaster and building peace across our region.

Ministry of Defence: mission, vision and commitment

Our mission

To defend and protect the sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Republic, assist and cooperate with other authorities in situations of emergency or disaster and restore peace in any part of Kenya affected by unrest or instability as assigned.

Our vision

A premier, credible and mission capable force deeply rooted in professionalism.

Our commitment

The Ministry of Defence is committed to defending and protecting the people of the Republic of Kenya and their property against external aggression and also in providing support to the Civil Authority as per the Law.

The national symbols

Article 9 of the Constitution names four national symbols: the flag, the anthem, the coat of arms and the public seal.

The flag

Black for the people, red for the blood shed in the struggle for independence, green for the land and white for peace. The shield and crossed spears stand for the defence of freedom. The national motto is Harambee, “let us all pull together”.

The national anthem

“Ee Mungu Nguvu Yetu” is a prayer for peace, unity and prosperity, sung at national and military occasions.

The coat of arms

The Republic’s principal emblem: a shield in the national colours with a cockerel holding an axe, supported by two lions holding spears, above the motto Harambee.

The public seal

The seal that authenticates the official acts of the Republic.

About the Ministry of Defence

How the Ministry and the Kenya Defence Forces are led and held to account.

Leadership of the Ministry

The Ministry is headed by the Cabinet Secretary, who is responsible to the President and Commander-in-Chief and to Parliament on matters of policy. The Cabinet Secretary is appointed by the President and chairs the Defence Council, which is responsible for the overall control and direction of the Kenya Defence Forces.

The Chief of the Defence Forces

The 2010 Constitution established the Kenya Defence Forces (KDF) under the Chief of the Defence Forces (CDF), who is the professional head of the three services, the Army, the Air Force and the Navy, and the principal military adviser to the Commander-in-Chief.

The CDF also advises the President and the Cabinet Secretary on military, operational and administrative matters. Subject to the general direction of the Defence Council, the CDF is responsible for the control, direction and general superintendence of the Defence Forces.

The Principal Secretary

The Principal Secretary is the accounting and authorised officer for financial and administrative matters of the Ministry, and is personally accountable to Parliament for all public money provided to it.

Mandate

The Ministry was created by Executive Order No. 2 of 2023, dated 1 November 2023, and its work is informed by the Constitution. It is obliged to facilitate and support the KDF in carrying out its mandate under Article 241(3)(a), (b) and (c) of the Constitution, so that the KDF, the Ministry’s technical arm, achieves its vision of being a premier, credible and mission capable force deeply rooted in professionalism.

Civilians in the Ministry

Civilian staff play a complementary role that lets the KDF concentrate on its core functions. Their numbers grew from about 200 in 1961 to more than 2,000 by 2020. They provide services ranging from policy, administration and financial management to public communications, human resource management, and medical and technical support.

Frequently asked questions

Who can apply?

Eligibility is set by each service and can change. This portal collects your details in advance but does not decide eligibility. Confirm the current requirements with the service when recruitment is announced.

Does pre-registering guarantee me a place?

No. It keeps your details ready so you are prepared. Selection happens only through the official recruitment process.

Which qualification should I choose and upload?

Choose your single highest level, KCPE, KCSE or tertiary, and upload that certificate: your KCPE or KCSE certificate, or your college or university certificate, diploma or degree, as a PDF, JPG or PNG up to 5 MB.

What do I bring to the recruitment office?

Your reference number, your original National ID and your original certificates. The full list will be confirmed by each service.

How is my information protected?

Your name, ID, phone number and certificate are encrypted when stored and used only for recruitment. A live version would follow Kenya’s Data Protection Act, with retention rules agreed in advance.

Can I use the portal in Kiswahili?

Yes. Use the Kiswahili button at the top right of the page to switch the language.
